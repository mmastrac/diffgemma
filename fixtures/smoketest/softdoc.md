# Kestrel Point Field Station — Season Log

Kestrel Point sits on the north shore of Bran Sound, on a shelf of grey rock
about forty minutes by boat from the harbour. The station has been in
continuous operation since the shelf was first surveyed, and the log below
covers the current season.

## Crew

The station is staffed by Iver Rusk, who serves as engineer and keeps the
generator in order; Wen Aliso, the hydrographer, who runs the sampling
programme; and Petra Combe, the radio operator. The radio operator is
responsible for the weekly summary sent up to Bran Harbour every Friday
evening, and for logging any traffic on the emergency channel.

Combe was the first to arrive this season, stepping off the tender on 2
March. Rusk followed on 9 March, and Aliso came last, on 20 March, after the
ice had cleared from the eastern approach.

There is also Mirren, a tabby who has adopted the station and who nobody
formally counts as staff. Mirren wears a mauve collar with a small brass tag
that gives the station's call sign.

## Power and structures

The generator sits in a lean-to on the leeward side of the main hut. It is
refuelled every fortnight from drums kept in the store, and Rusk logs the
hours after each run. The store itself is kept locked; only the engineer
holds a key to it.

The signal mast stands 12 metres above the deck and is guyed on four points.
It was re-tensioned at the start of the season and has held well since,
though the upper section hums badly in a hard easterly.

## Instruments and reporting

All of the station's instruments report on the hour, every hour, except the
tide gauge, which returns a reading only every six hours. This has been the
arrangement since the gauge was replaced, and the schedule is deliberate
rather than a fault: the gauge averages over a longer window and reporting it
more often would add nothing.

## Sampling programme

Aliso maintains three sampling sites in the sound. Site Bram lies furthest
out and drops to 47 metres. Site Crake, in the middle channel, reaches 31
metres. Site Marl, closest to shore, is the shallowest of the three at 18
metres. Each site is visited on a rotation, weather permitting.

## Season events

The mooring survey was carried out in March, in the first calm week after
Rusk arrived. The antenna repair came much later, in June, after the upper
guy had worked loose over a run of bad weather. Both jobs required the tender
and both were logged against the station's maintenance hours.

The winter resupply run, which the crew call the Long Haul, brings in fuel,
dry stores and any parts ordered during the season. It is the only visit of
the year that cannot be rescheduled around weather, and the crew plan the
autumn around it.

## Notes

The eastern approach silts up over the winter and should be sounded again
before the next season. The emergency channel was quiet all season apart from
one relay for a passing trawler. The lean-to roof needs re-felting.
